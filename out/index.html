01Why this exists

More than a learning platform. A community of builders.

Programming education rarely fails because there isn't enough material. It fails because watching, reading and collecting tutorials is not the same as understanding something well enough to use it.

We believe learning should move through six steps — and that most of them need other people. Code Yaar is being built as a practical learning platform. The founding community is where that belief is tested with real learners, from the beginning.

01

Understand

Work out how something actually behaves, not just what it is called.

02

Experiment

Change it, break it, and see what happens.

03

Practise

Repeat the small things until they stop needing thought.

04

Turn fragments of knowledge into something that runs.

05

Explain the work — the part that reveals what you missed.

06

Improve

Use feedback to make the next version better.

02Community principles

Five standards we hold each other to.

These are not slogans. They are the questions we ask when it is unclear what the right thing to do is.

  1. 1

    Build before you boast

    A rough project says more than a polished description. We value evidence of work over claims about ability.

  2. 2

    Learn by doing

    Courses and documentation are inputs. The output we care about is something you made, fixed or understood by trying.

  3. 3

    Share what you discover

    A solved error, a useful tool, a wrong assumption corrected — passing it on is how a small group learns faster than a large one.

  4. 4

    Give honest feedback

    Specific and kind, not vague and polite. Feedback is the fastest way for someone to improve, and the easiest thing to get wrong.

  5. 5

    Grow together

    Progress that only one person can see is half the value. We ask members to help the next person move forward.

03The First 100

The first hundred members of a community being built in the open.

The First 100 is the initial global founding community around Code Yaar — a small group of learners and aspiring developers who join before the platform is finished.

Founding members test early ideas, take part in practical activities as they become available, give feedback, and set the culture for everyone who joins later. They are participants, not an audience.

Read the full cohort details

What founding members are

  • A global cohort, assembled from open applications.
  • Beginners are the core audience, not an exception.
  • Asynchronous by design, so time zones do not decide who can take part.
  • Honest about what exists today and what is still being planned.

This is an early-stage initiative. The community is being assembled now, and activities will grow with the people in it.

06Community experience

How the community works

An eight-step journey from application to ongoing contribution.

  1. 01

    Apply

    Complete the five-step application. It takes about ten minutes.

  2. 02

    Review

    A team member reads your application against the cohort criteria.

  3. 03

    Welcome

    Selected applicants receive onboarding information by email.

  4. 04

    Connect

    Meet the other founding members and introduce yourself.

  5. 05

    Learn

    Take part in optional learning activities at your own pace.

  6. 06

    Build

    Work on practical challenges and projects as they become available.

  7. 07

    Contribute

    Share feedback and ideas that shape the community and the platform.

  8. 08

    Showcase

    Highlight projects and progress — including the messy parts.

This is the intended flow — some later stages depend on activities that are still being planned, and we will not pretend otherwise. Participation is asynchronous-first so that members can contribute from any time zone.

04What members can expect

Realistic benefits, stated plainly.

The founding cohort is early, so these are opportunities rather than guaranteed services. We would rather understate them than have members arrive expecting something that does not exist.

  • Early access to selected community experiences

    Founding members are the first to be invited as activities and community spaces open.

  • A say in early product decisions

    Structured feedback sessions where what you tell us can change what Code Yaar builds next.

  • Founding community discussions

    Conversations with other members who are learning and building at the same time as you.

  • Challenges and collaborative activities

    Practical programming activities invited on an opt-in basis, as they become available.

  • Project and progress showcases

    Opportunities to present what you have built, including work that is unfinished.

  • Founding member recognition

    Recognition is tied to participation. The exact format has not been confirmed yet.

What we do not promise. We do not promise jobs, internships, scholarships, professional certifications or career outcomes. Membership is not a credential, and joining does not guarantee acceptance into any future programme.
