# Community Activities

**Status:** Proposed catalogue. **No activity is currently scheduled or available.** Do not publish a calendar until the team confirms it can sustain it.

## Design constraints

1. **Async-first.** A member in UTC+8 and one in UTC−5 must both be able to participate fully.
2. **Small scopes.** An activity should fit in 2–4 hours across a week.
3. **Visible output.** Something another member can read, run or respond to.
4. **No prerequisite skill.** Beginners can attempt any activity.
5. **Sustainable cadence.** One reliable rhythm beats four irregular ones.

## Proposed activities

| Activity | Cadence (proposed) | Effort | Output | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **First build** — one tiny program from a prompt | Once, at onboarding | Low | A repo, snippet or screenshot | None |
| **Project showcase** — share what you made this month | Monthly | Low | Thread with links/screens | Low participation at first |
| **Practice challenge** — one problem, any language | Bi-weekly | Medium | Solution + short explanation | Review backlog |
| **Peer review exchange** — swap work, give structured feedback | Monthly | Medium | Written feedback | Feedback quality |
| **Technical discussion** — one topic, async thread | Weekly/bi-weekly | Low | Thread | Dying threads |
| **Code Yaar feedback session** — structured product questions | Per release | Low | Feedback summary | Feedback not visibly actioned |
| **Collaborative build** — small team, scoped feature | Quarterly, later | High | Shared project | Coordination overhead |
| **Learning log** — weekly "what I learned" | Weekly, optional | Low | Short posts | Feels like homework |

## Recommended launch order

1. First build (onboarding).
2. Project showcase (monthly).
3. Technical discussion (bi-weekly).
4. Practice challenge (only once review capacity exists).
5. Everything else later.

## Feedback protocol

For peer review, a structure that prevents both vagueness and harshness:

- **One thing that works.**
- **One thing that is unclear.**
- **One thing to try next.**

Reviewers respond to the work, not the person.

## Measuring activity health

Track attempts, submissions and responses per activity. If an activity attracts fewer than ~20% of active members twice in a row, retire it rather than defending it.

## What not to do

- Do not announce a schedule the team cannot keep.
- Do not create leaderboards or streak pressure.
- Do not require attendance at live sessions.
- Do not run activities that produce no shareable output.
- Do not launch five activities at once.
