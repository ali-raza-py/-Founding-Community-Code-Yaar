# Product Vision

**Status:** Confirmed direction, with clearly marked recommendations.

## The problem

Programming education is available. It is not always *coherent*.

A motivated learner typically moves between video courses, documentation, practice sites, chat threads and half-finished personal projects. Each resource is useful in isolation, but there is rarely a single environment where the learner can:

- ask a basic question without embarrassment,
- show work in progress to people who are at a similar stage,
- get feedback that is honest rather than polite,
- see what other beginners are actually building,
- and turn a series of tutorials into a body of practical work.

The result is a familiar pattern: high initial motivation, low completion, and a portfolio that never quite gets started. The missing piece is usually not more content. It is **a community with a practice-oriented culture**.

## Why a founding community is necessary

A community cannot be added at the end of a product roadmap. Its norms — how people ask questions, how feedback is given, whether showing unfinished work is normal — are established by the first members.

Starting with a small, deliberate founding cohort means:

- the culture is set by people who chose to be there early,
- expectations can be calibrated while the group is small,
- feedback about Code Yaar arrives from people who are genuinely invested,
- and growth later happens into an existing culture rather than defining one.

## Vision statement

> Build a global community of curious learners, aspiring developers and early builders who learn through practice, create meaningful projects, exchange knowledge, and contribute to shaping a more practical programming education experience.

## Mission

> Bring motivated learners together, encourage practical development, and create a community where people learn by doing and grow by contributing.

## The philosophy: Think. Build. Evolve.

| Word | Meaning in practice | How it shows up on the site |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Think** | Understand the problem before typing. Ask questions, reason about trade-offs, form an opinion. | Language that values reasoning and questions, not just output. |
| **Build** | Convert understanding into working, imperfect, real things. | Emphasis on projects, challenges and practical activity over completion badges. |
| **Evolve** | Improve from feedback, revise work, help the next person. | Honest feedback and contribution as stated community principles. |

The philosophy is a filter for decisions: if a feature, message or activity does not encourage thinking, building or improving, it does not belong.

## Long-term ambition

These are **directions**, not current services:

- collaborative programming challenges,
- community-led project showcases,
- peer learning sessions,
- technical discussions,
- shared learning resources,
- product feedback initiatives,
- community contributions to Code Yaar,
- collaboration on meaningful projects.

Nothing in this list may be described on the public site as already running. See [`02-product/CONTENT-STRATEGY.md`](../02-product/CONTENT-STRATEGY.md) for the tense rules that enforce this.

## Relationship between the community and the platform

```text
Code Yaar platform (separate repo, separate systems)
        │
        │  direction: practical, project-based programming education
        ▼
Founding community (this repo)
        │
        ├── brings motivated learners together early
        ├── runs practical, mostly asynchronous activities
        └── feeds structured feedback back to the platform team
```

The community is not a funnel metric and not a substitute for the product. It is where the culture of the product is tested with real people.

## What the community should become

- A place where a beginner's first question is answered properly.
- A place where finished work is shown, not just described.
- A group whose members can explain *why* they built something.
- A source of candid, structured feedback for Code Yaar.
- A community that stays useful when it is larger than 100 people.

## What it should not become

- A member-count contest or a leaderboard of follower counts.
- A certificate mill.
- A job-placement promise.
- A promotional channel that only broadcasts.
- A group that dies when the founding cohort stops being new.
- A second, competing education platform.

## Values that guide future decisions

1. **Practice over consumption.** Reward building.
2. **Honesty over hype.** No invented statistics, no imagined reach.
3. **Contribution over attendance.** Value the person who helps.
4. **Accessibility over exclusivity.** Beginners are the audience, not a tolerated minority.
5. **Sustainability over growth.** A small active community beats a large dormant one.

## Success, stated carefully

Success is not "10,000 members". Early success is: a cohort that shows up, finishes some work, gives usable feedback, and would recommend the community to a peer. Metrics are defined in [`01-strategy/SUCCESS-METRICS.md`](../01-strategy/SUCCESS-METRICS.md).
