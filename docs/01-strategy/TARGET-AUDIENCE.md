# Target Audience

**Status:** Confirmed by brief; ranking within the list is a recommendation.

## Who The First 100 is for

| Segment | Situation | What they need from us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Students in CS or related fields | Coursework covers theory; little practice with peers. | A place to build beyond assignments. |
| Beginners with some code exposure | Have finished a course or two, cannot yet build independently. | Structure, small scopes, permission to be a beginner. |
| Self-taught learners | Learn from docs, videos and trial and error, often alone. | Peers, feedback, a reason to finish things. |
| Aspiring developers | Preparing for a first role or first serious project. | Visible work and honest critique. |
| Early-stage builders | Have an idea or a side project, want collaborators' eyes. | Feedback and accountability. |
| Curious technologists | Adjacent to engineering, experimenting. | A low-pressure entry point. |

## Who it is not for

Being explicit protects the culture:

- People looking for a **job guarantee** or a hiring pipeline. None exists.
- People looking for a **certificate** to add to a résumé. None is issued (policy `TBD`; see FAQ).
- People who want to **sell** courses, services or audiences to members.
- People who want a **large audience** for content rather than a peer group.
- People who cannot commit to **any** participation — the cohort is small and the value is mutual.

## What we select for

Ranked by importance, with the review questions in brackets:

1. **Motivation that is specific** — can they say why *this* community, now? (Q: Why do you want to join?)
2. **Willingness to learn** — do they describe gaps honestly? (Q: What do you hope to learn?)
3. **Interest in practical work** — do they talk about building, not only consuming? (Q: projects, contribution)
4. **Collaboration mindset** — do they mention other people, feedback, helping? (Q: contribution)
5. **Realistic availability** — is the stated time plausible and honest? (Q: weekly time)
6. **Potential contribution** — anything from answering questions to writing docs to translating.

## What we explicitly do not select for

- existing technical skill level (beginner is the core audience),
- GitHub stars, follower counts or public portfolio size,
- institution prestige or a formal CS degree,
- nationality, region or country quotas,
- English fluency beyond being able to participate,
- ability to attend live sessions.

Optional links (GitHub, LinkedIn, portfolio) are context, not scoring inputs. An applicant without any of them is not disadvantaged. See [`../05-operations/APPLICATION-REVIEW-PROCESS.md`](../05-operations/APPLICATION-REVIEW-PROCESS.md).

## Age and minors

The brief does not set a minimum age. The form collects an **age range**, not a date of birth. If minors are admitted, a guardian-consent and safeguarding process is required *before onboarding*; that process is `TBD` and must be resolved before the cohort opens. See [`../06-privacy-and-safety/APPLICANT-SAFETY.md`](../06-privacy-and-safety/APPLICANT-SAFETY.md).

Until that policy exists, the safest public position is: applications from minors are reviewed under a process that is still being finalised, and acceptance for applicants under 18 depends on guardian consent. Do not state a minimum age publicly until it is decided.

## Language

The site is in English. Preferred communication language is collected in the form so the team can plan support; it is **not** a selection criterion.
