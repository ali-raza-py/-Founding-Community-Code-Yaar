# Founding Cohort Strategy

**Status:** Mixed — campaign definition is confirmed; process items are recommendations and marked `TBD`.

## Purpose of The First 100

1. **Set the culture** while the group is small enough to shape.
2. **Test the model** — do people actually show up for async practical activities?
3. **Generate real feedback** on Code Yaar's direction from invested early users.
4. **Produce visible work** that later members can look at and believe in.
5. **Create founding members** who can eventually lead activities themselves.

The First 100 is **not** a beta-testing programme with a fixed deliverable, and not a paid programme. It is the first 100 members of a community.

## Intended founding member profile

A strong founding member is not the most advanced applicant. They are:

- **Specific** about why they want this.
- **Consistent** at a modest level (for example a few hours a week, honestly stated).
- **Generous** — willing to answer a question or look at someone's work.
- **Curious** about how things work, not only about outcomes.
- **Realistic** — able to say what they do not know.

## Selection principles

| Principle | Implication |
| --- | --- |
| Motivation over achievement | A clear reason beats an impressive link. |
| Contribution over consumption | "What could you give?" is weighted above "what do you want?". |
| Consistency over intensity | 3 honest hours weekly is better than 20 claimed. |
| Diversity of stage | Beginners are necessary, not tolerated. |
| No geography quotas | Global by open application, not by allocated slots. |
| No automated rejection | Humans read every application. |
| No skill test | The cohort is not a hiring filter. |

The review framework is in [`../05-operations/APPLICATION-REVIEW-PROCESS.md`](../05-operations/APPLICATION-REVIEW-PROCESS.md).

**Nothing in stages 2–6 exists as a service yet.** The website may describe them as the process, using future/neutral language, and must not give dates. Response-time expectations must not be published until the team can meet them.

## Potential member benefits

Stated as *potential*, in this order of confidence:

| Benefit | Confidence | Public wording |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early access to selected community experiences | High | "Early access to selected community experiences." |
| Opportunity to influence early product decisions | High | "A say in early product decisions through feedback." |
| Founding community discussions | High | "Founding community discussions." |
| Challenges and collaborative activities *as they become available* | Medium | Keep the qualifier. |
| Project and progress showcases | Medium | Keep "opportunities to". |
| Founding member recognition *subject to participation* | Medium | Keep the condition. |

Never promised: jobs, internships, scholarships, certifications, guaranteed mentorship hours, or career outcomes.

## Member expectations

Willingness to participate constructively, respect others, share progress, give useful feedback, attempt practical activities, and communicate honestly about availability. No daily hours, no mandatory live attendance, no minimum contribution quota.

## Onboarding approach (proposed)

1. Selection notification with next steps.
2. Welcome message: what the community is, what it is not.
3. Community guidelines and code of conduct.
4. Short introduction thread (name, what you're learning, what you want to build).
5. Primary communication channel details.
6. A first small activity with a generous window.
7. A feedback prompt after two weeks.

All channel names, tools and dates are `TBD`.

## Recognition principles

- Recognition follows **demonstrated participation**, not joining.
- "Founding member" is a truthful label for the first cohort; it is not a rank or a credential.
- Do not create competitive leaderboards that reward volume over quality.
- Public showcases require the member's consent before publishing their work or name.

## Risks of overpromising

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Applicants expect a course and leave | Copy states plainly that this is a community, not a curriculum. |
| Applicants expect a certificate | FAQ answers "not currently decided" without implying one is coming. |
| Applicants expect a job pipeline | FAQ answers no, unambiguously. |
| Team cannot review at the implied speed | No response-time promise until the process is measured. |
| 100 accepted, 12 active | Design activities for small groups; treat inactivity as information, not failure. |
| Minors admitted without safeguards | Guardian-consent process required before onboarding. `TBD`. |

## Needs founder confirmation

- Certificate policy.
- Response-time target.
