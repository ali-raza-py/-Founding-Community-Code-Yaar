# Community Strategy

**Status:** Recommendation. Requires founder approval before being described as policy.

## Why the community exists

Covered in [`../00-overview/PRODUCT-VISION.md`](../00-overview/PRODUCT-VISION.md). Short version: programming learners lack a sustained environment for practice, feedback and shared progress. A community fixes the environment, not the curriculum.

## Target audience

Primary: students and self-taught learners who can write some code and want to build more. Secondary: early builders who want peers and feedback instead of another tutorial. Details and exclusions in [`TARGET-AUDIENCE.md`](TARGET-AUDIENCE.md).

## Global positioning

Open worldwide, asynchronous-first, honest about being new. Details in [`GLOBAL-POSITIONING.md`](GLOBAL-POSITIONING.md).

## Why people should join

Reasons that survive scrutiny — because they are about what a member does, not what they receive:

1. **A place to show unfinished work.** Most communities only reward finished things.
2. **Peers at a comparable stage.** Not an audience of experts, not a chat of absolute beginners.
3. **Structure without pressure.** Optional activities with clear scopes, not mandatory daily streaks.
4. **Influence on an early product.** Founding members see decisions being made and can argue with them.
5. **A habit of building.** The most common outcome of joining a good community is that you ship more.

Deliberately **not** promised: jobs, internships, certificates, scholarships, follower growth, or a portfolio review service.

## What members are expected to contribute

| Expectation | Realistic level |
| --- | --- |
| Participate constructively | Read, respond, act with respect. |
| Share learning progress | Occasionally, in whatever form is comfortable. |
| Give feedback | On activities and, when asked, on Code Yaar ideas. |
| Attempt practical work | At least some activities, at the member's own pace. |
| Communicate honestly | Including "I can't do this week". |

No mandatory hours. No attendance requirements. No quotas. A member who contributes one thoughtful review per month is more valuable than one who lurks daily.

## How the community creates value

```text
member activity  →  visible work  →  feedback  →  improvement  →  more confidence
       ↑                                                              │
       └────────────────  newcomers see it is possible  ──────────────┘
```

Value comes from **visible practice**. Every proposed activity should be judged on whether it produces something another member can see and respond to.

## Potential activities

All of the following are **planned possibilities**, not current services. None may be published with a schedule until the team can maintain it.

| Activity | Format | Effort to run | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Coding challenges | Async prompt, 1–2 weeks | Medium | Needs review capacity; avoid infinite backlogs. |
| Project showcases | Async thread or short demo call | Low–medium | Simplest high-value activity. |
| Peer learning sessions | Small group, optional live | Medium | Requires time-zone rotation. |
| Technical discussions | Async topic thread | Low | Good for low-effort weeks. |
| Feedback sessions | Structured questions about Code Yaar | Low | Output must be visibly acted on. |
| Collaborative building | Small teams, scoped project | High | Only after the cohort is stable. |

## Growth model

1. **Founding cohort (The First 100).** Small, hand-reviewed, culture-setting.
2. **Stabilise.** Prove that activities run on a predictable rhythm with current capacity.
3. **Open joining.** Introduce a lightweight way to join that does not depend on manual review.
4. **Delegate.** Members lead activities; the team shifts from running to supporting.

Growth is gated on **capacity**, not on demand. If the team cannot review applications or seed activities, opening the doors wider only dilutes the culture.

## Avoiding a dead member-count group

Explicit safeguards:

- **Cap before growth.** Do not exceed the number of members the team can give attention to.
- **Measure active contribution, not registrations.** See [`SUCCESS-METRICS.md`](SUCCESS-METRICS.md).
- **One reliable rhythm** beats five irregular ones. Start with the activity the team can sustain.
- **No channel sprawl.** One primary channel until there is a demonstrated need for a second.
- **Prune inactive members** after a stated period, with a friendly reactivation message. Mark the exact policy `TBD`.
- **Make joining a small commitment.** An application that requires thought filters better than a one-click join.
- **Publish what happened.** A monthly note of what members built and what changed keeps lurkers oriented.

## Community lifecycle states

A member moves through: `applied → accepted → onboarded → active → inactive → alumni`. The website only renders the first state (`applied`); the rest are operational and not implemented in this repository.
